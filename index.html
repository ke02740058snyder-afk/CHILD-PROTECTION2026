Every Child Deserves a Safe Childhood

Child protection is everyone's responsibility. Together, we can recognize the signs, take action, and build a safer world for the vulnerable children in our communities.

What is Child Protection?

Child protection refers to the policies, laws, and actions designed to safeguard children from violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect. It ensures that every child has the right to grow up in a safe, nurturing, and supportive environment.

Recognizing the Signs

Abuse isn't always visible. Knowing the warning signs can help you intervene and save a life. Look out for these indicators:

🩹

Physical Abuse

Unexplained bruises, burns, or fractures. The child may be wary of physical contact, flinch easily, or wear inappropriate clothing to hide injuries.

💔

Emotional Abuse

Extreme behaviors (overly compliant or aggressive), delayed development, sudden speech disorders, or appearing excessively fearful, anxious, or withdrawn.

🚸

Sexual Abuse

Age-inappropriate sexual knowledge, sudden changes in eating or sleeping, avoidance of specific people or places, or unexplained pain/infections.

🍽️

Neglect

Frequent absences from school, poor hygiene, untreated medical/dental issues, or being left unsupervised in dangerous situations.

What to Do If You Suspect Abuse

If you believe a child is being harmed, do not wait. Follow these steps to ensure their safety:

  1. Ensure Immediate Safety

    If the child is in immediate danger, call emergency services (911 or your local equivalent) right away. Do not confront the suspected abuser yourself.

  2. Listen and Reassure

    If a child discloses abuse to you, stay calm. Listen without interrupting, believe them, and reassure them that it is not their fault and you are there to help.

  3. Document What You Know

    Write down exactly what was said or observed, including dates, times, and specific details. Stick to the facts without adding your own interpretations.

  4. Report It

    Contact your local child protective services or a dedicated child abuse hotline. You do not need absolute proof to make a report; it is the job of professionals to investigate.
